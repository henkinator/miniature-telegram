# Side Hustle Plan

A living plan for building side income — covering three things: **earning extra money**, **building a side project into a product**, and **organizing ideas so you pick the right one**.

> **How to use this doc:** It's a template, not a finished answer. Fill in every `[...]` field with your own details, then work the steps top to bottom. Revisit it monthly.

## 3. Prioritize: score your shortlist

Score each shortlisted idea **1–5** on the five factors, then add them up. Highest total(s) win. Start with **one or two**, not five.

| Factor | What a 5 looks like |
|---|---|
| **Fit** | Plays directly to skills/interests you listed |
| **Low startup cost** | Needs little or no money to begin |
| **Speed to first €** | Could earn within days/weeks |
| **Income ceiling** | Can grow well beyond your goal |
| **Enjoyment** | You'd happily do it on a tired evening |

**Worked example:**

| Idea | Fit | Low cost | Speed | Ceiling | Enjoy | **Total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Freelance web dev | 5 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 3 | **20** |
| Small web tool (SaaS) | 4 | 4 | 2 | 5 | 5 | **20** |
| Tutoring | 3 | 5 | 5 | 2 | 3 | **18** |

> Ties are fine — pick the one you'll actually stick with. A common winning combo: a **service** for cash now + a **product** built on the side for scale later.

## 4. Guardrails before you start

- **Don't over-invest before your first €.** Validate demand with the cheapest possible version first.
- **Time-box experiments.** Give an idea a fixed trial (e.g. 4–6 weeks). If it's going nowhere, pivot — don't let sunk cost trap you.
- **Separate finances.** Track side-hustle money separately from day one; it makes tax time and decisions far easier.
- **Mind the basics.** Check local rules on registering income, invoicing, and tax for side earnings. (General reminder — not professional advice.)
- **Protect your main commitments.** Your studies/main job come first; the side hustle fits around them, not the reverse.

## 5. Side-project → product track

For the "build something" path. These are **checkpoints, not a calendar** — move to the next only when the current one is real.

1. **Validate demand.** Find 5–10 people with the problem. Will they say they'd pay? Pre-sell or collect sign-ups before building.
2. **Smallest shippable version (MVP).** Build the least that solves the core problem. Cut every "nice to have."
3. **Pick one launch channel.** Where your users already are (a subreddit, a Discord, a newsletter, a directory). One channel, done well.
4. **Get your first paying user.** One real payment teaches you more than 100 opinions.
5. **Iterate from feedback.** Talk to users, fix the biggest pain, repeat. Only then think about scaling or adding features.
